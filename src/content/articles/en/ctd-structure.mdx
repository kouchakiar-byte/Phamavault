The **Common Technical Document (CTD)** is the internationally agreed format for organizing registration dossiers, defined in the ICH **M4** guideline. It does not dictate *what* studies are required — that comes from regional rules and other guidelines — but it fixes *where* each piece of information goes, so assessors in different regions can navigate a dossier the same way.

## The five modules

The CTD is often drawn as a triangle: Module 1 is regional and sits outside it, Module 2 summarizes, and Modules 3–5 contain the underlying data.

| Module | Content | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Regional administrative information | Application forms, labelling, product information — defined by each authority, not harmonized |
| **2** | Summaries and overviews | Quality Overall Summary (2.3), non-clinical and clinical overviews and summaries (2.4–2.7) |
| **3** | Quality | Drug substance (3.2.S), drug product (3.2.P), appendices and regional information |
| **4** | Non-clinical study reports | Pharmacology, pharmacokinetics and toxicology |
| **5** | Clinical study reports | Including bioequivalence studies for generic products |

## Module 3 in more detail

For formulation and development teams, Module 3 is where most of the work lands:

- **3.2.S — Drug substance:** manufacturer, characterization, control of the API, reference standards, container closure and stability. Much of this can be supported by the API manufacturer's **DMF** or a **CEP**.
- **3.2.P — Drug product:** description and composition (3.2.P.1), **pharmaceutical development** (3.2.P.2), manufacture, control of excipients, control of the finished product, reference standards, container closure and stability.

Section 3.2.P.2 is where the development story is told: pre-formulation findings, the rationale for the chosen formulation and process, dissolution development and, where applicable, the Quality by Design elements described in ICH Q8.

## CTD vs. eCTD

The **eCTD** is the electronic transmission format for the same content, with a defined folder structure and XML backbone that supports lifecycle management of submissions. The content organization is the same; what changes is how it is packaged, submitted and updated.

## Practical tips

- Build the dossier structure **at the start of development**, not at the end — it acts as a checklist for missing data.
- Keep the Quality Overall Summary (2.3) consistent with Module 3; discrepancies between them are a common source of questions.
- For generic products, Module 4 is usually not required, and Module 5 centres on the bioequivalence study or a justified biowaiver.
