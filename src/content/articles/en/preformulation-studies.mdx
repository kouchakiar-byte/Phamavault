Pre-formulation is the systematic characterization of a drug substance's physical, chemical and mechanical properties **before** a dosage form is designed. Its purpose is simple: replace trial and error with data, so that formulation and process choices are made for a reason.

## Why pre-formulation matters

Most late-stage development problems — poor dissolution, unstable products, tablets that cap or stick — trace back to a property of the API that was not understood early enough. A focused pre-formulation package typically answers three questions:

1. **Can the drug dissolve and be absorbed** from the intended dosage form?
2. **Is the drug stable**, alone and in contact with candidate excipients?
3. **Can the drug be processed** reliably at scale?

## Key parameters to characterize

| Property | Typical methods | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solubility vs. pH (1.2–6.8) | Shake-flask, HPLC/UV | Dissolution risk, BCS class |
| pKa, log P / log D | Potentiometric titration, partitioning | Ionization and permeability |
| Polymorphism / solid form | XRPD, DSC, TGA | Solubility and stability differences between forms |
| Hygroscopicity | Dynamic vapour sorption (DVS) | Packaging, process humidity control |
| Particle size & morphology | Laser diffraction, microscopy | Content uniformity, dissolution rate |
| Flow & compressibility | Bulk/tapped density, Carr index, Hausner ratio | Choice of direct compression vs. granulation |
| Intrinsic stability | Forced degradation (acid, base, oxidation, heat, light) | Degradation pathways, stability-indicating methods |

## Drug–excipient compatibility

Compatibility screening exposes binary mixtures of the API with candidate excipients to stress conditions (for example elevated temperature and humidity) and compares them with the pure API. **DSC** is a fast screening tool, but thermal events can be misleading; results should be confirmed by **isothermal stress testing with HPLC** for assay and degradation products.

Common interactions to watch for include the Maillard reaction between primary or secondary amines and reducing sugars such as lactose, and oxidation catalysed by peroxide residues in polymers such as povidone or crospovidone.

## From data to decisions

A well-documented pre-formulation report connects each finding to a development decision:

- Poor aqueous solubility (BCS II/IV) points toward particle size reduction, solid dispersions or solubilizing excipients.
- Poor flow and low dose favour wet or dry granulation over direct compression.
- Moisture sensitivity drives the choice of excipients, manufacturing environment and packaging.

> Pre-formulation data also feeds directly into the **Pharmaceutical Development** section of the CTD (3.2.P.2), so documenting it well pays off twice.
